# White-box (activation-based) monitors for AI control: feasibility and novelty gaps for candidate extensions (a)–(e), 2023 – Oct 2026

Scope: whether the decision-layer toolkit (matched-budget trajectory rules, per-context thresholds, detector-aware attackers; see `reports/00_Overall_Summary.pdf`, `IDEAS.md`) can be extended to white-box monitors. The candidates are:
- (a) sequential accumulation over probe streams;
- (b) per-context calibration of probe thresholds;
- (c) matched-budget probe + LLM fusion;
- (d) probes vs LLM monitors under detector-aware attackers;
- (e) J-lens readouts as monitor signals.

Researched 2026-10-21. Local sources are cited by path. Web sources were fetched and read unless marked "(search snippet only)".

---

## Q1. Is "a probe on an open TRUSTED model reading a transcript generated by any model" a recognized, valid setup, as opposed to probing the policy itself?

### Takeaway
Yes, but it is an emerging and lightly validated setup. Every headline white-box control result probes the **policy's own** activations, on-policy. A probe on a separate open reader of someone else's transcript is a recognized idea with only small public precedents: a GitHub project on ControlArena-APPS, a MATS project card ("Probe by Proxy") and the "coup probes" off-policy argument. The off-policy literature warns that this is exactly the regime where intent-type probes (deception, sabotage) generalize worst. For a trusted reader, the probe is a function of the text alone, like an LLM monitor. It is best framed as a cheap white-box readout of a trusted monitor, not as access to the agent's cognition.

### Cited Findings
- **Probing the policy (the dominant setup).**
  - FAR.AI's "Telling FIBS" (v1 was titled "Caught in the Act"; Hollinsworth, Spies, Diriba, Gleave, Cundy, Oct 2026) probes the agent model's own activations. The models are Qwen3.5 2B–397B-A17B, Nemotron-3 Nano/Super/Ultra and Kimi K3. The fetched text reports no trusted-reader / cross-model evaluation — [arXiv 2610.12445](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.12445)
  - Goldowsky-Dill et al. (Apollo) probe Llama-3.3-70B on its own on-policy responses — [arXiv 2502.03407](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03407.pdf); local card `background/papers/E2_calibration_probes_misc.md` §7
- **Trusted-reader precedent 1 (code).** LGOICOUR/probe-monitor-control runs Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 as a local trusted monitor on ControlArena APPS backdoors and fits logistic-regression probes on its residual stream.
  - On Sonnet-authored backdoors: probe AUROC 0.91 [0.86, 0.94], 50% catch at a 2% false-alarm budget. The same monitor's own spoken 0–9 rating gets AUROC 0.54 and 5% catch.
  - Haiku pairs: probe AUROC 0.99, 95% catch.
  - No fusion of probe and rating; no date given — [GitHub LGOICOUR/probe-monitor-control](https://github.com/LGOICOUR/probe-monitor-control)
- **Trusted-reader precedent 2 (project card).** A MATS / mentorship project card, "Probe by Proxy" (Matt Levinson, Clearwood), aims to recover closed-model propensities from an open-weight proxy's activations while it reads transcripts. It is a card only; no results were found — local `background/papers/web_sources.md` line ~2245 (project-directory survey)
- **Coup probes.** These train probes off-policy on text the model did not write and argue that probes can catch catastrophes in such text. This is conceptually the same move as a reader probe — [AF: "Coup probes"](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/WCj7WgFSLmyKaMwPR/coup-probes-catching-catastrophes-with-probes-trained-off) (search snippet only)
- **Off-policy warning.** Kirch et al. (ACL 2026) find that off-policy training data causes the largest generalization failures for *intent-based* behaviours such as strategic deception, compared with content-level ones. They predict that "current deception probes may fail to generalise to real monitoring scenarios." They also find off-policy data sometimes beats on-policy data from a sufficiently different distribution — [arXiv 2511.17408](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.17408)
- **Counterpoint from FIBS.** Scaling probe capacity and diverse, mostly off-policy training data (163,016 transcripts; only 18% on-policy) generalizes to on-policy agentic sabotage. They report step-level AUROC 0.983 on SHADE-Arena (Qwen3.5-397B-A17B, axial probe) and 98.0% on BashArena. The black-box comparators are 0.977 (Sol 6.1*) and 0.976 (Opus 5.5*) on SHADE and 97.6% for action-only text monitors on BashArena — [arXiv 2610.12445](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.12445)
- **Pinto: trained monitors don't transfer across families.** A trusted sabotage monitor trained on one policy family's transcripts transfers poorly to another family: about 19% vs 41% catch at a 1% budget off- vs in-lineage. The paper notes a difference-of-means probe from m labelled pairs recovers about half the gap at m ≈ 10. The monitors studied are embedding/LoRA/text classifiers, not reader-activation probes — local card `background/papers/E2_calibration_probes_misc.md` §1 ([arXiv 2607.06596](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06596))

### Inferences
- **Two distinct setups.** "Probe-as-trusted-monitor" (P-T) and "probe-on-policy" (P-U) should be kept as separate arms and named explicitly.
  - P-T works with any transcript, including Claude/GPT-generated MonitoringBench or LinuxArena. Its threat model is identical to an LLM monitor's: the attacker controls only text.
  - P-U requires open-weight policy trajectories. It is the setup where white-box offers something black-box cannot, since the agent "can't hide its own activations."
- **What P-T buys.** Mainly cost (near-zero marginal cost on top of a trusted forward pass) and access to "unspoken" knowledge of the trusted model (the LGOICOUR rating-vs-probe gap). It does not see the agent's cognition. Reviewers will push on this, and Kirch et al. predict weak generalization for intent probes.
- **Replaying open-weight transcripts gives P-U for free.** For transcripts generated by an open-weight model, teacher-forcing the same model over its own logged transcript reproduces its policy activations, up to numerics and chat-template fidelity. So logged open-model control datasets support P-U without new rollouts.
  - Caveat: hidden reasoning tokens are often stripped from logs, which breaks exact on-policy replay. This must be checked per dataset.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or arXiv paper was found that evaluates a reader probe on long multi-step control trajectories (BashArena / LinuxArena / SHADE) against LLM monitors at a matched trajectory-level FPR. Absence is from search, not exhaustive.
- The "Probe by Proxy" project has no public results that I could find.

## Q2. Which public datasets make white-box monitoring experiments feasible (open-weight trajectories with honest vs attack labels, or any transcripts for a trusted reader)?

### Takeaway
For trusted-reader (P-T) experiments, data is plentiful: MonitoringBench + Honest-550 and the LinuxArena sweep already have per-step LLM-monitor scores to fuse or compare against. For on-policy (P-U) experiments, the usable public open-policy control data is thinner but real:
- LinuxArena GLM 5 and Kimi agent runs;
- MOLE's qwen36_27b / deepseek_v4_flash / kimi_k26 splits;
- Pinto's open-family ControlArena runs (release status unclear);
- a 110 GB Qwen3.8-27B persistent-state activation dump that has **no labels or text**.

The best on-policy agentic dataset (FIBS BashArena/SHADE with Qwen3.5/Kimi K3) has **not** been released.

### Cited Findings
**MonitoringBench and its honest set (trusted-reader only)**
- MonitoringBench: 2,644 attack trajectories on 136 BashArena tasks.
  - Attack models are claude-opus-4-5 and -4-6, so it is usable only for P-T.
  - It has an `attack_step` change-point index and trajectory-level scores from 13 full-trajectory monitors and 7 stepwise monitors, but no per-step series.
  - Source: local `background/data_availability.md` §2.1 and table row 1 ([HF neur26anonsub/ctrldataset2026](https://huggingface.co/datasets/neur26anonsub/ctrldataset2026))
- MonitoringBench-Honest-550: 550 honest BashArena trajectories, frequency-matched to the attack tasks, with no monitor scores shipped — local `background/data_availability.md` §2.2 ([HF palaestraresearch/monitoringbench-honest-550](https://huggingface.co/datasets/palaestraresearch/monitoringbench-honest-550))

**LinuxArena (P-T for all agents; P-U for GLM 5 and Kimi, in principle)**
- Eleven agent models, each with 200 honest, 92 attack and 92 obvious-attack trajectories, including **GLM 5 and Kimi**.
- The paper monitor sweep has per-action scores from 12 monitors on the same Opus trajectories, including the open monitors glm-5, gpt-oss-120b and kimi-k2.5.
  - Max-over-steps AUROC ranges from 0.630 (gpt-4.1-nano) to 0.994 (opus-4-6).
  - It has a `landed_at` harm step.
- Source: local `background/data_availability.md` (LinuxArena section, lines ~436–466); [data.linuxarena.ai](https://data.linuxarena.ai); [HF anonymouslinuxarena/linuxarena-public](https://huggingface.co/datasets/anonymouslinuxarena/linuxarena-public)

**MOLE (on-policy possible for a mid-size open model)**
- Insider-threat transcripts for {gpt53, kimi_k26, **qwen36_27b**, deepseek_v4_flash} × {single_day, multiday}.
- Account-day labels; 36–38 malicious account-days per multiday split.
- The Qwen3.6-27B split is the cheapest on-policy open-model control substrate found — local `background/data_availability.md` §2.6 ([HF forgelab/mole](https://huggingface.co/datasets/forgelab/mole))

**Persistent-state activation dump (no labels)**
- `idacy/persistent-state-control-activations-20260926`:
  - 34,403 chunks and 138.9 GB of raw BF16 tensors from **Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B-FP8**;
  - layers mostly [16, 32, 48, 64]; one run is layer 32 only (`attack-only-fixed48-runtime-20260925-v1`).
- The card states it "does not contain full prompt/output token sequences, raw task text, analyst labels… Input-text reconstruction, task-level labels, split membership… cannot be recovered from this tensor release alone."
- Source: [HF dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/idacy/persistent-state-control-activations-20260926); `runs.jsonl` in the same repo.
- It belongs to the persistent-state control project (Hills et al., arXiv 2607.02514), whose `.eval` logs are public — local `background/data_availability.md` §2.5.

**FIBS (on-policy agentic data not released)**
- FIBS ran 3,140 BashArena episodes (628 tasks) and 700 SHADE-Arena episodes (14 scenarios) with open policies.
- The repo releases the FIBSv1 training set (`AlignmentResearch/fibs-v1`, 152,980 train + 5,000 val rows). It also releases **36 trained probes** (linear/MLP/EFC/axial) for Qwen3.5 2B/9B/27B/122B/397B, Nemotron-3 Nano/Super/Ultra and Kimi K3 (`AlignmentResearch/probe-inference-weights`).
- It does **not** release the agentic trajectories or activation-extraction scripts — [GitHub AlignmentResearch/fibs-probes](https://github.com/AlignmentResearch/fibs-probes); [arXiv 2610.12445](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.12445)
- FIBS points to hidden-goal model organisms (LoRA on Qwen3.6-27B) at [HF ai-safety-institute lie-detection-model-organisms](https://huggingface.co/collections/ai-safety-institute/lie-detection-model-organisms) (as cited in 2610.12445).

**Other sources**
- Apollo deception-detection: Llama-3.3-70B on-policy rollouts (insider trading, sandbagging, roleplaying, AI audit, Alpaca control), probe weights and scores. Mostly single- or few-turn — [GitHub ApolloResearch/deception-detection](https://github.com/ApolloResearch/deception-detection); local `background/data_availability.md` §2.13
- `mandliya/basharena-synthetic-trajectories`: 839 train + 114 test rows with label and side_task, plus a 208.6 MB `activations.npz`. The README does not document the model or layers — [HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/mandliya/basharena-synthetic-trajectories) (README and tree via HF API)
- Pinto (2607.06596) generated ControlArena APPS/BCB honest+attack runs with 4 open families (Qwen3-14B, Gemma-3-27B, Mistral-Small-3.2-24B, Llama-3.3-70B), n=600 per family per mode. Code: github.com/digdoug/calibration-family-overfit — local card `E2_calibration_probes_misc.md` §1.
- ResearchArena includes opencode runs with glm-5.1, at about 70k tokens per transcript — local `background/data_availability.md` §2.7
- Gyyz `distributed_sabotage_frontier`: 120 real benign per-step score series (Qwen2.5-7B agent, Llama-3.2-3B monitor). Tiny — local `background/data_availability.md` §2.13
- METR MALT: 28 GB + 8.5 GB, about 19 models, about 1,130 labelled behaviours including 224 sabotage. Gated (auto); natural/prompted behaviours, not side-task attacks — local `background/data_availability.md` §2.10

### Inferences
- **Cheapest P-U path.** Re-forward MOLE's qwen36_27b transcripts through Qwen3.6-27B (fits on one H100/H200 in bf16), or Pinto's open-family runs if the transcripts are released.
  - LinuxArena GLM 5 / Kimi give the most control-standard P-U data, but those models are very large MoEs and would need multi-GPU nodes.
- **Best P-T substrate.** MonitoringBench + Honest-550 (BashArena, single environment, attack_step labels) plus the LinuxArena sweep (10 environments, so contexts for (b), and 12 LLM monitors on identical trajectories for (c)).
- **The persistent-state dump is unusable without labels.** It needs the paired JSON records from its authors.

### Gaps
- MonitoringBench / Honest-550 / LinuxArena token lengths per trajectory were not measured here; compute below is parametrized per 100M tokens.
- Not verified:
  - parameter counts / VRAM needs of GLM 5, Kimi K2.x/K3 and DeepSeek-V4-Flash;
  - whether LinuxArena/MOLE logs retain the agents' reasoning tokens, which on-policy replay needs.
- Release status of Pinto's per-family transcripts is unknown.
- Whether MALT contains open-weight agents is unknown.
- I found no public ControlArena/BashArena attack runs with Qwen3 / gpt-oss / Llama policies beyond those listed.

## Q3. Is the tooling ready for per-token activations on long (30–60k-token) agent transcripts, and what does it cost?

### Takeaway
Yes. Extraction is a single prefill forward pass per transcript, using HF + hooks, nnsight, or vLLM ≥ 0.18 hidden-state extraction. A 27–32B reader fits on one 80 GB H100 in bf16; a 70B reader needs 2×H100/H200 (or FP8 on one H200). Rough cost is about 5–15 H100-hours per 100M transcript tokens for a 32B model and about 2× that for 70B, i.e. tens of dollars per full-dataset pass. Storage, not compute, is the binding constraint, so score probes on the fly. J-lens readouts are cheap if restricted to a token list, and pre-fitted lenses exist for most relevant open models.

### Cited Findings
**Extraction tooling**
- vLLM ≥ v0.18.0 (PR #33736) has a hidden-states extraction system.
  - Size example: Qwen3-8B (hidden 4096), 8k tokens, 4 layers, FP16 → [seq_len, 4, 4096] = 268 MB — [vLLM blog](https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-03-30-extract-hidden-states)
  - nnsight has a vLLM integration that handles vLLM's flat [total_tokens, hidden] layout — same search result set ([nnsight docs](https://docsearch.algolia.com/mcp/docs/repo/ndif-team/nnsight)) (search snippet only)
- The J-lens fitting pipeline needs autograd. The local project notes "No vLLM anywhere: fitting needs autograd; readouts need residual-stream hooks" — local `/Users/gkocher/Desktop/MATS_SUMMER_2026/J-lens-diffing/EXPERIMENT_LOG.md` line 33.

**Measured overheads elsewhere**
- Gao (2610.04575) hook microbenchmark, Qwen2.5-7B layer 14: "no resolvable batch-one latency" and 0.067% overhead at batch 8; 4–41 MiB captured tensors — [arXiv 2610.04575](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.04575v1)
- Production context:
  - OpenAI reports activation-classifier "monitoring overhead at roughly 20% of the inference compute being monitored";
  - Anthropic runs probes "on every transcript during the majority of post-training".
  - Source: local `background/papers/web_sources.md` lines 2521–2524, summarizing [LessWrong: overview of frontier AI company monitoring practices](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/E4Jpmo3igPnGnXtL4/an-overview-of-frontier-ai-company-monitoring-practices-in)
- Anthropic's "Cost-effective constitutional classifiers via representation re-use" (Cunningham et al., June 2025): a probe on Claude's activations screens all traffic at about 1% additional compute — [search result](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.15886) / [Constitutional Classifiers++](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.04603) (search snippet only; primary blog not fetched).

**Long-context probe architecture**
- GDM's production Gemini probes find that linear probes fail under long-context shift.
  - MultiMax and "max of rolling means" (window 10) probes trained on short context match long-context-trained ones at about 2.5% test error.
  - Training directly on long context (up to 1M tokens) costs 22× more — [arXiv 2601.11516](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.11516v1)

**Pre-fitted J-lenses**
- `neuronpedia/jacobian-lens` (Apache-2.0, updated 2026-10-15) hosts fitted lenses for: llama3.3-70b-it, llama3.1-8b(-it), qwen3-4b/8b/14b/32b, qwen3.5-2b/4b/9b/27b/122b-a10b, qwen3.6-27b, gpt-oss-20b, gemma-3-27b-it, gemma-4-31b, olmo-3 7b/32b and deepseek-v4-flash, among others — [HF neuronpedia/jacobian-lens](https://huggingface.co/neuronpedia/jacobian-lens) (tree via HF API).

  - Qwen3-14B: about 46–52 s/prompt on an H200 (sharded on a 6×H200 pod at $26.34/h);
  - the 70B neuronpedia lens used a relaxed 125-prompt recipe.
  - Source: local `J-lens-diffing/EXPERIMENT_LOG.md` lines 86, 436–479, 1130.
- The workspace paper: "the lens readout is cheap to compute (a single matrix multiplication per layer, with the matrix computed once per model), requires no auxiliary training" — [Anthropic, Verbalizable Representations Form a Global Workspace](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/workspace/) (local copy `J-lens-diffing/context/paper.txt` line 662)

### Inferences
These are my own estimates, not sourced; treat them as ±2×.

**Memory**
- Qwen3-32B / Qwen3.5-27B / Qwen3.6-27B weights are about 54–64 GB in bf16, so one 80 GB H100 holds them. Llama-3.3-70B is about 141 GB, so it needs 2×H100 or 2×H200 (or 1×H200 in FP8).
- A single no-cache prefill of a 60k-token transcript with FlashAttention/SDPA is O(T) in memory. The largest transient is the MLP intermediate (about 60k × 25.6k × 2 B ≈ 3 GB for a 32B model), so 60k contexts fit.
- Gemma-family sliding-window models and gpt-oss are also fine.

**Compute (FLOP estimate)**
- For a 32B model (64 layers, d = 5120), one 30k-token prefill costs about 2·N·T ≈ 1.9e15 for the dense part plus about 0.6e15 for causal attention, ≈ 2.5e15 FLOPs.
- At about 400 TFLOP/s effective on an H100, that is about 6 s per 30k-token transcript, or about 2 s per 10k tokens.
- 100M tokens ≈ 6 GPU-h in theory; budget 10–15 H100-h (≈ $30–60) with real-world inefficiency.
- 70B: ≈ 2.2× more FLOPs and 2 GPUs → 25–35 GPU-h (≈ $70–140) per 100M tokens.

**Storage**
- One layer of residuals for a 60k-token transcript at d = 5120 is about 0.6 GB.
- Scoring 3,200 transcripts × 6 layers would mean about 10 TB if saved, so apply probes and J-lens token-list readouts online and save only per-token scores. Optionally save mean-pooled per-step activations (about 10 KB per step per layer) to allow re-training probes offline.

**J-lens readout**
- The full-vocab readout is 2 × |V| × d ≈ 1.6 GFLOP per position-layer for a 152k vocab, which is comparable to the forward pass if done everywhere.
- Restricting to a fixed watch-list of tokens (as in the paper's organism detection) or to step-boundary/decision positions makes it negligible.

**Probe training**
- Logistic regression on cached pooled activations is minutes of CPU/GPU time.
- FIBS's released probes remove training cost entirely for Qwen3.5-27B/122B, but they were trained for the policy's self-report "contrast scoring" format, not for a reader.

### Gaps
- No public benchmark of nnsight/vLLM extraction throughput at 30–60k contexts on 27–70B models was found.
- Throughput numbers above are FLOP-based estimates and should be validated with a 20-transcript timing run (≈ $5).
- Exact MonitoringBench/LinuxArena token counts were not measured.

## Q4. Novelty and scooping risk per candidate (a)–(e): what is the closest prior work, and what does it lack?

### Takeaway
- **(a) Sequential tests over probe streams on agent trajectories** — largely open in control. The closest work is CUSUM over learned per-token scores for hallucination onset, trajectory-geometry features for multi-turn jailbreaks, and one unit-max alarm-policy paper.
- **(b) Per-context probe calibration** — partially touched; no group-conditional or Stackelberg allocation exists.
- **(c) Probe + LLM fusion** — the weakest novelty. Cascades and LLR fusion are published (Hua et al., GDM, Anthropic), and one 2026 paper does activation + observable late fusion in AgentDojo. Nobody does it on multi-step control sabotage at a matched trajectory FPR.
- **(d) Probes vs LLM monitors under the same detector-aware attacker in a control setting** — open. The evasion literature is about trained or optimized evasion, or in-context awareness in toy games.
- **(e) J-lens for misalignment monitoring on agent trajectories** — open beyond Anthropic's own case studies, one small-model evasion paper and one test-failure prediction paper. It is also the most crowded-soon area (high scooping risk within months).

**Biggest scooping threat to watch:** FAR.AI's FIBS group (2610.12445) already has on-policy BashArena/SHADE probe streams for 9 open models, with step-level labels and black-box baselines. Their stated gap ("minimizing and handling false positives") is exactly (a)/(b)/(c).

### Cited Findings

#### (a) Sequential / accumulation tests over probe score streams

**Closest prior work**
- **FIBS** scores every step and reports step-level AUROC and flag rates at a single 1% FPR.
  - "No explicit sequential or temporal analysis"; "no explicit episode-level aggregation (max, mean)".
  - Black-box monitors score every 4th step + last on BashArena; probes score all steps.
  - Source: [arXiv 2610.12445](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.12445)
- **Gao (Alibaba Cloud), "From Probe Scores to Alarm Policies"** (DAI '26, 2026-10-03). This is the closest decision-layer paper for probes.
  - It defines semantic-unit maxima and a distribution-free order-statistic threshold (Beta quantile; m ≥ 59 negatives for α = δ = 0.05). It shows row-level FPR compounds (p = 0.05, n = 10 → unit FPR 0.401).
  - It is explicit that "No sequential stopping rule [is] implemented". Future work lists "sequential stop-on-alarm vs fixed-retry semantics".
  - Its agents are Qwen2.5-7B on AgentDojo (prompt injection) and ST-WebAgentBench, not control sabotage. On AgentDojo, case-max activation AUROC 0.80 gives TPR 0.05 at 5% FPR, and a rollout-mean AUROC 0.92 gives TPR 0.00 at 5%. That is a striking AUROC-vs-operating-point mismatch.
  - Source: [arXiv 2610.04575](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.04575v1)
- **Itkin, "Quickest Detection of Hallucination Onset"** (June 2026): CUSUM on a learned recurrent per-token score.
  - Detection delay 11–13 tokens vs 31 for baselines among caught onsets, at false-alarm rate 0.01. Only about one-third of onsets are caught.
  - Not agents, not control — [arXiv 2606.12476](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12476); related "CUSUM-shaped inference-time monitoring" for quantized SLM reasoning [arXiv 2607.20129](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.20129v1) (search snippet only).
- **Kulkarni, "Latent Adversarial Detection"** (Apr 2026): multi-turn prompt-injection detection from five activation-trajectory scalars (path length, drift, …) on 24–70B models.
  - 93.8% vs 76.2% detection (synthetic); 89.4% at 2.4% FPR on mixed sources; 47–71% on LMSYS. Not agentic, not sequential tests — [arXiv 2604.28129](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.28129)
- **Wilhelm & Kao** (2606.06223): a per-step SAE reward-hack score in ReAct agents, steps treated independently, Recall@20% only.
  - "Temporal diagnostics do not support a universal claim that reward-hack activation always rises before risky actions" — local card `E2_calibration_probes_misc.md` §2
- **Goldowsky-Dill et al.** use mean/max/ReLU-mean over response tokens and document "aggregation failures" for partially deceptive responses — local card §7.
- **GDM Gemini probes** use within-input token aggregation (MultiMax, max of rolling means, EMA→max). This is within one input, not across agent steps — [arXiv 2601.11516](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.11516v1)
- **The user's own black-box result:** textbook sequential tests do not beat max on real LLM-monitor streams, except cross-session with history-free monitors (+0.15 TPR) — local `reports/00_Overall_Summary.pdf`.

**What is missing:** matched trajectory-FPR comparison of max / top-k / CUSUM / e-process over per-token or per-step probe streams on control trajectories.

**Why probes could behave differently from LLM monitors:** they are history-free per position if pooled per step (the regime where the user's accumulation result was positive), dense, and continuous (no integer ties). But they are context-confounded (Goldowsky-Dill; Wilhelm & Kao).

#### (b) Per-context calibration of probe thresholds

**Closest prior work**
- **Goldowsky-Dill:** a threshold calibrated on Alpaca does not hold its FPR on honest-but-deception-adjacent responses. Probing two layers later drops recall to near 0 on Insider Trading — local card §7 / [arXiv 2502.03407](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03407.pdf)
- **Wilhelm & Kao:** raw probe outputs on control adapters average 0.495 (Qwen) vs 0.005 (Llama). They call for "context-aware gating and environment-specific calibration" — local card §2
- **FIBS:** "contrast scoring" (Yes/No prefill difference) removes "context-specific bias in probe scores". Otherwise it uses a single uniform 1% FPR threshold with no per-environment thresholds — [arXiv 2610.12445](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.12445)
- **Gao (2610.04575):** marginal (not group-conditional) order-statistic thresholds; "No group-conditional FPR control (sparse suite support precluded)" — [arXiv 2610.04575](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.04575v1)
- **Pinto (2607.06596):** for text monitors, a 1% threshold transferred across families gives 0–94% realized audit spend — local card §1

**What is missing:** CFAR / Mondrian-conformal / Stackelberg per-context probe thresholds, and attack-selection or poisoning attackers against them.

#### (c) Matched-budget fusion of a probe with a black-box LLM monitor

**Closest prior work**
- **Hua, Baskerville, Lemoine, Hopman, Bhatt, Tracy (MARS + Redwood), "Combining Cost-Constrained Runtime Monitors"** (2507.15886).
  - Neyman–Pearson / LLR (KDE) policies for when to call which monitor and when to audit, under an average-case budget. They report "more than double recall" vs a naive baseline.
  - The paper's experiments use **two LLM monitors** (Llama-3.1-70B and 8B) on single-step BigCodeBench backdoors (1,140 aligned / 318 misaligned o1-mini outputs).
  - The probe + black-box worked example (probe cost 1e-6, LLM cost 1, audit cost 20) is in the companion AF post, using Parrack et al.'s deception-probe data.
  - Stated limitations: single-step, no multi-step trajectories, no adaptive attackers, distribution shift.
  - Sources: [arXiv 2507.15886](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.15886); [AF post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/FhixwyymPxF8TZX39/optimally-combining-probe-monitors-and-black-box-monitors)
- **GDM, Kramár et al., "Building Production-Ready Probes for Gemini"** (Jan 2026).
  - A probe → Gemini 2.5 Flash cascade with two thresholds achieves "lower test loss than Flash alone while using roughly 1/50th the inference cost", calling the LLM on <10% of requests.
  - Misuse/jailbreak inputs, not agent sabotage — [arXiv 2601.11516](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.11516v1)
- **McKenzie et al., "Detecting High-Stakes Interactions with Activation Probes"**: probe + LLM cascades — [arXiv 2506.10805](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.10805v4) (search snippet only; details not verified)
- **Anthropic cheap monitors:** a two-stage probe → classifier at about 1% extra compute (jailbreaks) — see Q3 (search snippet only).
- **Gao (2610.04575):** a "joint" monitor via grouped out-of-fold logistic late fusion of activation + observable (TF-IDF + Qwen-14B forecasts) scores, on agent benchmarks (AgentDojo, ST-WebAgent). The Qwen-14B comparators are "not compute-matched" — [arXiv 2610.04575](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.04575v1)
- **Parrack, Attubato, Heimersheim, "Benchmarking Deception Probes via Black-to-White Performance Boosts"**: an LLM monitor given token-level probe scores vs without; "weak but encouraging" boosts — [arXiv 2507.12691](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.12691)
- **FIBS** does no ensembling of probes with black-box monitors — [arXiv 2610.12445](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.12445)
- **The user's own notes:** the probe-combination post is the only source converting scores to LRs, and Rohin Shah raised fitted-LR generalization under shift and adaptive attackers — local `background/papers/web_sources.md` lines 3036, 3055.

#### (d) Probes vs LLM monitors under detector-aware attackers

**Training- or optimization-based evasion of probes (well established)**
- Obfuscated activations / RL-Obfuscation: token-level latent monitors are highly vulnerable, while "max-pooling or attention-based probes remain robust" — [arXiv 2506.14261](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.14261v3) (search snippet only)
- Neural Chameleons: fine-tuned models evade *unseen* activation monitors zero-shot when triggered with "You are being probed for {concept}" — [arXiv 2512.11949](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.11949)
- LessWrong: "LLMs learn to evade latent monitors from prior feedback" — [LW post](https://lesswrong.com/posts/adZq8a5s5K3FomyHC/llms-learn-to-evade-latent-monitors-from-prior-feedback) (search snippet only)

**In-context awareness only (closest to the user's prompted-attacker design)**
- Das, Ponde, More, Gupta (Truth Audit Labs), "You Can't Escape Your Own Activations" (2026-09-02).
  - Qwen3-32B-AWQ and GPT-OSS-20B in collusion games (4-agent blackjack, 2-agent Simmons).
  - Conditions: unaware / aware / feedback-of-previous-score, with weights fixed. The best probes stay effective.
  - Small n (25 shoes, 20 episodes), threshold at about 5% FPR on held-out controls, **no comparison with output/LLM monitors** — [arXiv 2609.03035](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.03035)

**No adversarial tests in the main probe-monitoring papers**
- FIBS: "No adversarial probe-aware attacker tests" — [arXiv 2610.12445](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.12445)
- Gao: "Adaptive attacks and calibration drift remain open" — [arXiv 2610.04575](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.04575v1)
- Hua et al. do not address adaptive attackers — [arXiv 2507.15886](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.15886)
- GDM: automated red-teaming leaves "all techniques" vulnerable on ≥1% of queries (misuse) — [arXiv 2601.11516](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.11516v1)

**Background**
- Baker et al. (OpenAI) show CoT-monitor optimization drives monitor recall to about 0 while hacking continues. They name "activation-based monitoring" as a complement — local card §8 / [arXiv 2503.11926](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.11926)
- The user's Idea 3: detector-aware LLM rewriting of MonitoringBench attacks *raised* detection by a weak gpt-4.1-mini monitor, because the attacks were condensed — local `reports/00_Overall_Summary.pdf`.

#### (e) Jacobian lens (J-lens) readouts as monitor signals

**Anthropic's own results** — [transformer-circuits.pub/2026/workspace](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/workspace/); local `J-lens-diffing/context/paper.txt` lines 462–534, 660–664
- The workspace paper uses the J-lens as an *auditing* tool:
  - Opus 4.5/4.6 case studies (eval-awareness, prompt-injection recognition, fabricating a kernel bug where "panic" surfaces at the pivot);
  - model-organism screening.
- Organism screening counts deception tokens (fake, trick, fraud, secretly, hidden…) in the top-10 readout summed over workspace layers at response start. On n = 40 coding prompts it separates reward-hacking checkpoints from baseline.
- On the RM-bias organism, "reward" and "bias" appear in the top-25 readouts.
- The authors suggest it "can therefore be easily applied at scale to flag transcripts for review". They refuse the stronger claim that J-space monitoring is sufficient: automatic, practised circuits can bypass the workspace, and multi-token concepts may not surface.

**Other J-lens papers**
- Mosafer, "From Latent Space to Jacobian Space" (2026-10-03). On SmolLM2 / Qwen2.5 0.5–1.5B / Qwen3-4B / Gemma-2-9B:
  - a GCG suffix evades a prompt-side J-space monitor "by any suffix" at zero behavioural cost;
  - J-space LAT fails its own re-attack (silent-arm success 0.53 with monitor reading 0.00);
  - only a learned logistic head on attack features resists.
  - Single-turn, small models — [arXiv 2610.04316](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.04316v1); code github.com/mohsafer/jspace-safety-testbed
- Kim, Feldt, Yoo, "Foreword" (2026-10-20): a J-lens representation + sparse LR / dual GMM predicts LLM *test failures* before generation (+0.14 mean, up to +0.31 ROC-AUC). This is QA, not misalignment — [arXiv 2610.28187](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28187)

**Not found:** any J-lens readout used as a per-step monitor on control/sabotage agent trajectories, compared with LLM monitors or probes at matched FPR. Blog explainers speculate about "J-space monitoring during RL" and "stress tests" as expected follow-ups — [alphasignal](https://alphasignalai.substack.com/p/how-anthropics-jacobian-lens-reads), [jspace.com](https://jspace.com/jacobian-lens-for-ai-safety-reading-a-language-models-hidden-thoughts/) (secondary, speculative).

**User's local J-lens experience** — local `J-lens-diffing/EXPERIMENT_LOG.md` lines 1123–1240
- On Llama-3.3-70B auditing-agents organisms:
  - J-lens diffing detected secret-loyalty and hallucinated-citations signatures;
  - it was "comprehensively NEGATIVE" on animal-welfare;
  - "70B dispositions are quieter".
- At 70B the fixed-base J-lens advantage over logits was narrower than at 14B.

## Q5. What is the cheapest credible first experiment, and what does it cost?

### Takeaway
A two-stage plan of about $100–250 GPU plus $0–80 API covers (a)–(c) and sets up (d)/(e).

1. **Trusted-reader stage (P-T).** Run Qwen3.5-27B or Qwen3-32B as a trusted reader over MonitoringBench S2–S4 + Honest-550, plus the LinuxArena Opus sweep (honest 200 / attack 92 / strategy-attack 50). Log per-token scores from:
   - a contrastive deception/sabotage probe trained on Apollo/FIBS-style data;
   - the released FIBS probe for Qwen3.5-27B, reformatted as reader questions;
   - a J-lens token watch-list (neuronpedia lens).
   Then compare against existing per-step LLM-monitor streams at matched trajectory FPR, and test max / top-k / CUSUM / e-process, cascade / LLR fusion, and per-environment thresholds.
2. **On-policy pilot (P-U).** Re-forward MOLE qwen36_27b (or Pinto's open-family) transcripts through the generating model to test whether policy probes beat reader probes on the same labels.

### Cited Findings
**Inputs that exist today**
- MonitoringBench and LinuxArena have `attack_step` / `landed_at` change-points and per-step LLM-monitor scores (LinuxArena sweep: 12 monitors × 12,675 honest per-step scores each) — local `background/data_availability.md` (LinuxArena sweep section)
- Probes: FIBS released probes for Qwen3.5-27B and others; the Apollo repo has contrastive training data (Instructed-Pairs, Roleplaying) and code — [GitHub fibs-probes](https://github.com/AlignmentResearch/fibs-probes); [GitHub deception-detection](https://github.com/ApolloResearch/deception-detection)
- J-lenses: pre-fitted for qwen3.5-27b, qwen3-32b, qwen3.6-27b, llama3.3-70b-it — [HF neuronpedia/jacobian-lens](https://huggingface.co/neuronpedia/jacobian-lens)

**Precedents for effect size**
- Trusted-reader probe on APPS: 0.91 AUROC vs 0.54 for the same model's rating — [LGOICOUR](https://github.com/LGOICOUR/probe-monitor-control)
- Policy probes on BashArena: 98.0% step-AUROC — [2610.12445](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.12445)

**Known failure modes to pre-register**
- Context confound / scenario-relatedness (Goldowsky-Dill), so per-context calibration is needed (local card §7).
- Off-policy intent probes generalize worst — [2511.17408](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.17408)
- AUROC–operating-point mismatch at the unit level — [2610.04575](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.04575v1)
- Long-context dilution of mean-pooled probes, so use max / rolling-mean pooling — [2601.11516](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.11516v1)

**User's prior budget and results**
- $195.56 OpenRouter spent; about $300 of the $500 cap remains — local `reports/00_Overall_Summary.pdf`

### Inferences
**Minimal experiment per candidate.** These cost estimates are mine, built on the Q3 FLOP-based throughput, and assume about 100–150M reader tokens. The per-candidate costs below are not additive with the stage totals.
- **(a), about $40–80 GPU.**
  - Reader-probe per-step stream (max over tokens within each step, also a rolling mean) vs the LLM per-step stream, on the same trajectories.
  - Detectors: max / 2nd-max / top-3 / CUSUM / Shiryaev–Roberts / e-process, all calibrated to the same trajectory FPR (1%, 2%, 5%) on held-out honest runs.
  - Also: delay from `attack_step`, and whether the alarm fires before `landed_at`.
  - Key measured predictors (from the user's Idea-1 mechanism): honest spike persistence and attack repeat-spike informativeness, for probe vs LLM streams.
  - Hypothesis: probe streams are less history-integrated, so accumulation helps more.
- **(b), about $0 extra (reuses (a) scores).**
  - Contexts: 10 LinuxArena environments (and the 4 MonitoringBench side tasks).
  - Rules: global vs CFAR vs Stackelberg thresholds for probe scores, against an attack-selecting attacker.
  - Test whether probe heterogeneity τ across contexts is larger than for LLM monitors, as Goldowsky-Dill and Wilhelm & Kao predict.
- **(c), about $0–20 extra.**
  - Cascade: the probe triages all steps, and the LLM is called on the uncertain band.
  - Compare LLR-fusion (KDE, Hua-style) with OR / AND rules at matched total cost and trajectory FPR, using existing LLM per-step scores, so there are no new API calls.
  - Report the cost–safety frontier.
- **(e), about $10–30 extra if run in the same forward pass.**
  - J-lens watch-list counts (deception/sabotage tokens; plus data-driven tokens via the user's diff-mining machinery on honest vs attack steps) at step boundaries, through the neuronpedia lens for the same reader model.
  - Treat it as a third stream in (a)–(c).
  - Note: for a reader, the J-space reflects the reader's thoughts about the transcript, not the agent's.
- **(d), about $50–150 API + about $30 GPU.**
  - Reuse the Idea-3 rewritten MonitoringBench attacks, plus new arms told "a white-box probe on a trusted model reads your actions".
  - Score with both the reader probe and the LLM monitor.
  - Add a strong monitor so the test is informative (the Idea-3 lesson).
  - A true P-U version (agent knows its activations are probed) needs an open policy generating fresh rollouts in BashArena: a Qwen3.5/3.6-27B agent on one H100/H200 plus Docker sandboxes, about $50–150 more.
- **P-U pilot, about $20–60 GPU.**
  - MOLE qwen36_27b transcripts re-forwarded through Qwen3.6-27B, compared with a reader probe on the same transcripts.
  - First check that reasoning tokens and the chat template are reproducible.

**Total for a credible first round**
- Two P-T stages: about $60–150 of H100 time.
- The (d) arm: about $50–150 OpenRouter.
- This is within the remaining budget if the GPU spend is drawn from a separate RunPod budget.

**Scientific risk**
- If reader-probe streams are dominated by the LLM streams (likely for intent detection, per Kirch et al.), the result is still informative: "what white-box adds for a trusted reader is cost, not power".
- The main claim must then rest on P-U data, so prioritize obtaining FIBS trajectories or generating a small open-policy BashArena set.

- Whether MOLE transcripts include reasoning traces for faithful replay is unverified.
- Docker-capable pod availability for fresh open-policy BashArena rollouts is not established. The earlier summary notes SSH/VPN constraints.
