# Prior work already half-scoops sequential AI monitors

AI control and agent monitoring as practiced from 2023 to October 2026 is almost entirely **one-shot Neyman–Pearson testing against a pooled honest baseline**. Each action or trajectory gets an LLM suspicion score. The audit threshold sits at the 98th–99.9th percentile of honest-run scores, chosen to match a human audit budget, and trajectories are usually aggregated by max. Deployed products (Apollo Watcher Live, Claude Code auto mode, OpenAI's internal monitor) use fixed severity cutoffs instead. Sequential evidence accumulation is **no longer unexplored**. Wen et al. (ICLR 2025) run a Bayesian likelihood accumulation across tasks. Ctrl-Z uses a three-strikes count rule. Claude Code auto mode has a session denial counter. Stateful cross-PR and cross-account monitors appeared in 2026. One June 2026 preprint (Zhang & Gao) explicitly benchmarks **CUSUM and SPRT on AI-control suspicion streams**, though only on synthetic score traces. Adjacent fields already use CUSUM on LLM signals (SafeDream for multi-turn jailbreaks, CPD Online for adversarial suffixes, CURA and Dubey for agent failures). CPD Online even pairs a per-deployment robust baseline with CUSUM, which is structurally CFAR plus CUSUM. The genuinely open piece is narrower. No published AI-control work **sets thresholds from each task's or environment's own honest baseline (CFAR-style)**. None reports **ARL, detection delay or false-alarm guarantees** for real LLM-monitor score streams on BashArena, LinuxArena or SHADE-Arena. None tests such monitors against **attackers who know the accumulator**. The red team against the idea is strong. Accumulation adds nothing against single-action catastrophes. CUSUM is defeated by attackers who pace below its drift term. Constant-FPR-per-context thresholds hand a selective attacker a map of where detection is weakest. Per-context baselines can be poisoned by the very model being monitored. My verdict: **scooping risk is high for "sequential statistics beat max-aggregation on distributed sabotage", low-to-moderate for a CFAR-normalized, ARL-calibrated, adversarially evaluated monitor on real control settings**. That window is closing fast, because GDM, UK AISI and UPenn groups are all working the adjacent problem.

## Every lab thresholds a pooled honest quantile, reset per unit

The research-evaluation convention is uniform across organizations. Greenblatt et al.'s original AI Control paper picks "a classifier threshold such that the classifier has a 2% false positive rate on the distribution of solutions written by GPT-4" ([Greenblatt et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06942)). The agentic successors keep the same rule at tighter budgets:

- **Ctrl-Z:** 1% of actions ([Ctrl-Z](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10374)).
- **BashArena:** 4% trajectory-wise, about 0.08% step-wise ([BashArena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.15688)).
- **LinuxArena:** the "99th percentile of the honest per-action score distribution… A trajectory counts as flagged if any of its actions is flagged" ([LinuxArena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15384)).
- **Apollo MonitoringBench:** the 99th percentile over 416 honest BashArena runs ([MonitoringBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09684)).
- **UK AISI Async Control:** the 99th percentile of a random-forest aggregator's output ([Async Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.13526)).
- **Anthropic SHADE-Arena:** a cutoff of 80/100, chosen for roughly 1% FPR ([SHADE-Arena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.15740)).
- **GDM's CoT-necessity work:** an approximately 1% FPR ([Emmons et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.05246)).
- **METR's MALT validation:** recall at 5% FPR ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2025-10-14-malt-dataset-of-natural-and-prompted-behaviors/)).

The metrics follow suit: TPR at fixed FPR, AUROC, and safety-versus-usefulness at an audit budget. **No source reported detection delay, average run length (ARL), or false alarms per deployment-hour** for any agent monitor. Redwood's own estimate of realistic budgets is around 1e-4, "1 audit for every 10,000 actions" ([Redwood Logit ROCs](https://blog.redwoodresearch.org/p/logit-rocs-monitor-tpr-is-linear)). That tail regime is exactly where empirical quantiles are least stable.

Deployed systems swap quantiles for absolute cutoffs:

- **Apollo Watcher Live** cascades an auto-approve stage, a triage LLM and a gateway LLM, with "severity threshold 6" at both LLM stages. Users can override these thresholds and supply organizational boundaries through the prompt ([Watcher Live](https://watcher.apolloresearch.ai/blog/announcing-watcher-live/)). Context therefore enters through the rubric, not through a per-context score baseline.
- **Claude Code auto mode** runs a two-stage binary classifier (stage-1 FPR 8.5%, full-pipeline FPR 0.4%). It escalates when "a session accumulates 3 consecutive denials or 20 total" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/claude-code-auto-mode)). That is the only published deployed mechanism that accumulates evidence over a session, and it is a hand-set count rule.
- **OpenAI's internal monitor** assigns per-conversation severity levels within 30 minutes. Over tens of millions of trajectories, about 1,000 moderate alerts were escalated and none reached the highest level ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/how-we-monitor-internal-coding-agents-misalignment)).
- **Anthropic's internal monitors** are reported at the level of rates. The online monitor blocks about 1 in 47,000 actions. The offline monitor, aimed at "dangerous behaviors that develop slowly," flags 1–2 per 1,000 transcripts, but no cross-transcript statistic is described ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/institute/measuring-pace-of-ai-development)).

Adaptivity does exist, but at the **protocol** level, not in the detector statistic. Redwood-lineage work built several such mechanisms:

- **Exponentially decaying audit thresholds** in Ctrl-Z nearly doubled trusted-monitoring safety, from **22% to 41.8%** ([Ctrl-Z](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10374)).
- **Control Tax** proves that non-increasing audit allocations weakly dominate ([Control Tax](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05296)).
- **The original AI Control paper** ran a binomial running test (p = 0.03 against an expected 2%) on the false-positive count. It switches to the trusted model and back, which works like hysteresis ([Greenblatt et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06942)).
- **Hua et al.** audit on KDE-estimated likelihood ratios across monitors. That is the Neyman–Pearson building block of SPRT, applied once per output ([Hua et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15886)).

Several 2025–2026 benchmarks deliberately went back to constant thresholds for comparability. Factor(T,U) restricts the blue team to "a constant audit threshold for all tasks" ([Factor(T,U)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02157)). The most-cited current numbers are therefore fixed-FPR, max-aggregated numbers. A benchmark that reports only TPR at fixed FPR cannot credit a sequential detector at all unless the metric changes.

The fixed-quantile convention has visible cracks, and these motivate the idea:

- **Per-step FPR compounds across a trajectory.** In MonitoringBench, "a 1% per-action FPR compounds to an effective trajectory-level FPR of 6.7–17.4% across monitors." Per-monitor thresholds on the same honest set range from **4.00 to 8.00** on a 0–10 scale ([MonitoringBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09684)).
- **Discrete scales degenerate at low FPR.** MonitoringBench's 0–100 prompt variant produced a threshold of 100/100 and **a 0% catch rate** at 1% FPR. BashArena notes that its monitor gave 9/9 to 9 of 9,894 honest actions, so it cannot reach FPRs below about 0.09% ([BashArena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.15688)).
- **Max-aggregation raises the honest threshold on long trajectories**, even when mean suspicion stays low ([Control OSWorld](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03818v1)).
- **Scoring errors dominate strong-monitor failures.** MonitoringBench attributes 82% of one strong monitor's failures to "scoring or calibration errors," driven by a threshold of 8.97 ([MonitoringBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09684)).

## Sequential statistics already reached AI control, on synthetic streams

Two other items deserve precise reading because their names invite confusion. The "sequential monitors" of Chen Yueh-Han et al. for decomposition attacks are an LLM reading the cumulative prompt history, not a statistical sequential test ([Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10949)). The "sequential" scaffolding of Kale et al. means recursive chunk summarization ([Kale et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19461)). TRACE likewise accumulates evidence through an LLM Triage–Inspect–Judge loop on SHADE-Arena, not through a statistic ([TRACE](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07054v1)).

The single most threatening paper is Zhang & Gao. It claims to be "the first formal treatment" of the gap. It is a roughly 10-page, non-peer-reviewed preprint, but it occupies the headline claim. It also makes a point any follow-up must absorb: "Classical CUSUM and SPRT are designed for mean shifts. When applied to the per-step scores of a marginal-preserving attack, they detect nothing" ([Zhang & Gao](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.10456)). Their sequence monitors work by exploiting autocorrelation, not mean shift. Wen et al. are the conceptual ancestor. Their update is additive in log-odds (they note "Bayesian updates are additive in log-odds space"), which makes it a multi-hypothesis likelihood-ratio accumulator. Their own text also documents the failure mode a CFAR input would target: "Naively following the Bayesian update procedure causes disastrous miscalibration when the reference distribution is imperfect" ([Wen et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.17693)). One source discrepancy remains unresolved. The ICLR abstract reports 99.6% usefulness, while the full-text version reports 98.4% usefulness, 99.2% safety, and 80% fewer backdoors than non-adaptive baselines ([ICLR 2025](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/699c5a2bb87a8aa32c4ad541d9997361-Abstract-Conference.html); [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2411.17693)).

Outside AI, the method is decades old, so no statistical novelty exists to claim:

- **Network security** used nonparametric CUSUM for SYN floods in 2002 ([Wang, Zhang & Shin](https://rtcl.eecs.umich.edu/rtclweb/assets/publications/2002/wang2002detecting.pdf)) and Wald's SPRT for port scans in 2004 ([Jung et al.](https://people.csail.mit.edu/awberger/papers/fastPortscanDetectionUsingSequentialHypothesisTesting_JungPaxsonBergerBalakrishnan_2004.pdf)).
- **Commercial SIEMs** ship per-entity baselines. Microsoft Sentinel UEBA builds each entity's baseline from "its own historical activities, those of its peers, and those of the organization as a whole" ([Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-in/azUre/sentinel/identify-threats-with-entity-behavior-analytics)).
- **Splunk risk-based alerting** sums per-entity risk over a sliding window ([Splunk](https://help.splunk.com/en/splunk-enterprise-security-8/administer/8.6/risk-based-alerting/risk-scoring-in-splunk-enterprise-security)).
- **LLM providers** already aggregate misuse evidence per account, through hierarchical "summaries of summaries" ([Anthropic](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2025/summarization-for-monitoring/)) and Clio's cross-account clustering ([Clio](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13678v1)). Neither describes a formal test.

## Per-context CFAR baselines remain unbuilt in control, though widely requested

Component (a) is where the literature is thinnest. In AI control, calibration happens per monitor (MonitoringBench) or per attacker model against "its model-matched honest run" (LinuxArena). It never happens per task or per environment at runtime ([LinuxArena](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.15384)). SHADE-Arena considered per-agent thresholds and **rejected** them, because "the 99th percentile is a high variance statistic… making it safer to set a single threshold across all agents" ([SHADE-Arena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.15740)). The idea has been **suggested** several times without being tested:

- **Kale et al.:** "a dynamic threshold based on the environments and the knowledge about the agent under monitoring may work even better" ([Kale et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19461)).
- **Bruce W. Lee's 2026 agenda** lists "calibration transfer across settings" and warns that "a threshold tuned on APPS Backdooring will be meaningless in a researcher's ML codebase" ([Salient Directions in AI Control](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/kitaRHhSzphdvqie2/salient-directions-in-ai-control)).
- **GDM's roadmap** endorses UEBA, described as "establishing a baseline of normal activity for users and network entities… and continuously monitoring for deviations" ([GDM roadmap](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.13087v1)).

The term "constant false alarm rate" does not appear in any LLM-monitoring paper found.

The empirical case that honest score distributions differ by context is real but indirect:

- **Context type:** the full-diffs monitor in the persistent-state study "sees the entire accumulated diff, so its honest scores run higher and its thresholds saturate" ([Hills et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.02514)).
- **Deployment transfer:** Dubey's monitor drops to chance when moved across deployments without recalibration ([Dubey](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.02464v1)).
- **Model lineage:** Pinto reports a cross-family AUROC interaction of +0.172, but this is abstract-level evidence only ([Pinto](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06596)).

**No one has published per-environment honest-score distributions** for BashArena, LinuxArena or SHADE-Arena. Whether CFAR has any heterogeneity to exploit in standard control settings is therefore untested. That is the first experiment the idea needs, and it could kill the idea.

## The red team: why the idea may fail or add little

**Accumulation adds nothing against concentrated catastrophes.** If one action suffices, that action's score is all the evidence there is, and a top-quantile threshold already is the Neyman–Pearson test. Spending audit budget on accumulated alarms takes budget away from the single-step tail. Roger and Shlegeris's toy model finds the optimal lab response to rare concentrated attacks is to "spend all the auditing budget at the start" ([Toy models of AI control](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/MDeGts4Aw9DktCkXw/toy-models-of-ai-control-for-concentrated-catastrophe)). That front-loading is already a time-varying threshold, and already published. Within-trajectory multi-step attacks are real: Ctrl-Z's best attack "spreads short malicious commands over 10 steps" ([Ctrl-Z post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/LPHMMMZFAWog6ty5x/ctrl-z-controlling-ai-agents-via-resampling)). But that slot is already filled by three strikes, time travel, and SHADE-Arena's max over the last 20% of turns. For diffuse threats, Redwood's own framing cuts against threshold accumulation. Without an accurate picture of how suspicious benign models look, "no amount of mildly suspicious actions is enough to incriminate the model" ([Hebbar](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/Mf5Hnpi2KcqZdmFDq/how-can-we-solve-diffuse-threats-like-research-sabotage-with)). Anthropic-affiliated authors argue that "training interventions seem more promising than monitoring and incrimination for diffuse threats" ([Diffuse AI Control](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/diffuse-ai-control/)).

**A monitor-aware attacker defeats CUSUM by design.** Control evaluations assume Kerckhoffs's principle: the attacker knows "the monitor's identity, algorithms, and decision thresholds" ([Salient Directions](https://lesswrong.com/posts/kitaRHhSzphdvqie2/salient-directions-in-ai-control)). Three results show what that implies for CUSUM:

- **Pacing below the drift term.** Any per-step excess at or below the reference value k accumulates nothing. Urbina et al. derive the optimal stealthy attacker against CUSUM in industrial control systems: it drives the statistic to the threshold and then injects exactly b per step forever ([Urbina et al.](https://users.soe.ucsc.edu/%7Ealacarde/papers/ccs16.pdf)).
- **Covert adversaries in theory.** Ramtin, Nain and Towsley show that an adversary who knows the false-alarm parameter γ can force CUSUM detection delay to **Θ(γ) rather than O(log γ)** (abstract-level) ([Ramtin et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20022v2)).
- **Resets and dilution.** CUSUM's reset floor lets an attacker interleave honest steps and wipe accumulated evidence. Ctrl-Z tried a distribution-level test across a trajectory (Kolmogorov–Smirnov) and found it "little better than just looking at the current step, and in fact… quite brittle when subject to targeted red-teaming" ([Ctrl-Z post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/LPHMMMZFAWog6ty5x/ctrl-z-controlling-ai-agents-via-resampling)). Max-type rules dominate the control literature precisely because adding benign content cannot dilute them.

The counterweight is real. Urbina et al. also find that "stateful tests are better in limiting the impact of stealthy attackers (for the same levels of false alarms)". That argument depends on harm scaling with per-step deviation. In AI control, one subtle backdoor line can be catastrophic while carrying almost no statistical signal.

**CFAR is not minimax, and its baseline can be poisoned.** Constant FPR per context raises thresholds where honest scores are high or noisy, such as security-adjacent tasks, sysadmin work and long sessions. Those are the contexts where an attack is easiest to disguise. Attack selection already costs **20–28 percentage points of safety** at a 1% audit budget on BashArena and LinuxArena with global thresholds ([Ge-Wang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06529)). Publishing per-context thresholds tells a selective attacker where to strike. In a Stackelberg audit game, the defender should equalize the attacker's expected payoff across contexts, not the honest FPR, as game-theoretic IDS threshold work makes explicit ([Ghafouri et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.06707)). Worse, the untrusted model generates its own "honest" reference data. Async Control already warns that "the untrusted model can try to influence monitor calibration by producing 'honest' episodes" ([Async Control](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.13526)). The security precedent is unambiguous. In ANTIDOTE's "Boiling Frog" experiments, **5% weekly chaff growth pushed a retrained detector's false-negative rate from 3.67% to 50% within three weeks** ([ANTIDOTE](https://ptolemy.berkeley.edu/projects/truststc/pubs/725/IMC.2009.pdf)). The radar version is target masking, where interfering targets in the reference cells inflate the threshold. Here the monitored agent chooses what enters the reference window ([IJEECS CFAR comparison](https://ijeecs.iaescore.com/index.php/IJEECS/article/download/20542/13623)).

**The statistics are fragile on real monitor outputs.** Estimating a 1%-FPR threshold per context needs hundreds of honest samples per cell. LinuxArena's 1,671 tasks spread over 20 environments leave a few hundred correlated actions per environment at best ([LinuxArena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15384)). In practice, per-context cells collapse to shrinkage toward the global threshold, which is a modest variance-reduction trick. Integer 0–10 scores with heavy ties break both per-context quantiles and the continuous ARL approximations used to set CUSUM thresholds. Within-trajectory scores share context and are autocorrelated. Once thresholds are re-fit empirically on honest trajectories to fix the resulting false alarms, CUSUM becomes "an aggregation function with an honest-quantile threshold", which is current practice. The post-change distribution is chosen by the attacker, so CUSUM's optimality guarantees do not transfer.

**Strong baselines already compete.** Schirmer et al. found that a well-calibrated single threshold is "competitive with more advanced monitors based on sequential hypothesis testing" on LLM outputs ([Schirmer et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.02510)). Full-history LLM monitors can link semantically related steps, such as a download at step 3 and an execution at step 9, which a scalar sum cannot represent. A paper showing CUSUM beats max on a fixed set of non-adaptive attack trajectories will be read as an aggregation ablation.

**The field is moving quickly.** At least six May–October 2026 papers attack distributed and multi-agent sabotage: SCHEME, UPenn stateful monitoring, AISI FakeLab, Hills et al., MiniLab, and Zhang & Gao ([MiniLab](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.24052); [SCHEME](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.29178)). MiniLab finds per-agent monitors flag only 7% of successful distributed attacks versus 87% of single-agent ones (abstract-level). Anytime-valid statistics now have their own NeurIPS 2026 workshop, where the exfiltration e-process paper is an oral ([Ortega-Fernandez et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11843)). This sweep could not search ICLR 2027 OpenReview submissions or workshop-only papers, so an unseen scoop is plausible.

## Verdict: narrow novelty survives where measurement and adversaries meet

Scooping risk differs sharply by sub-claim:

| Claim | Scooping risk | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Sequential statistics beat max/mean against distributed sabotage | **High** | Zhang & Gao (synthetic); stateful monitors from Brown et al. and Hills et al. |
| CUSUM over multi-turn LLM risk | **High** | SafeDream |
| Bayesian cross-task credence on the untrusted model | **Taken** | Wen et al. |
| CUSUM / e-process on agent trajectories (non-adversarial) | **High** | CURA, Dubey, E-valuator |
| Per-entity accumulation for misuse | **Moderate-high** | Brown et al.; Anthropic summarization; Clio |
| CFAR per-task/per-environment honest-baseline thresholds in AI control | **Low** | Suggested by Kale, Lee, GDM; built only in adjacent domains (CPD, CURA, Dubey) |
| CFAR-normalized input → CUSUM/SPRT/e-process → ARL-calibrated alarm, on real control settings, against accumulator-aware attackers | **Low-moderate, time-sensitive** | Nearest structural twin is CPD Online (jailbreaks); nearest in-domain is Zhang & Gao (synthetic) |

Four angles remain open, in order of value.

**First: measure the heterogeneity before building anything.** The first deliverable is per-environment honest suspicion-score distributions across LinuxArena's 20 environments and SHADE-Arena's domains, together with within-trajectory autocorrelation. If per-environment 99th percentiles differ by less than a score point, CFAR is moot. If they differ by several points, the result alone is publishable, and nobody has reported it.

**Second: reframe CFAR as budget stability rather than detection power.** The defensible benefit of per-context normalization is keeping the realized honest audit rate on target under drift, new repositories and model updates. That cost is quantified by the 6.7–17.4% trajectory-FPR compounding and the transfer failures documented above. A credible result would show a global threshold mis-spending the audit budget on a long, shifting honest deployment, and a robust, trusted-sourced per-context baseline holding the budget.

**Third: move accumulation to where it uniquely helps.** That is cross-session and cross-instance, model-level incrimination in persistent-state and multi-agent settings. These are already runnable on FakeLab, MOLE and Iterative VibeCoding ([MOLE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06966)). The right metric there is Urbina's damage achievable while undetected at a fixed false-alarm rate, plus detection delay before harm lands. GDM explicitly names this as an open problem.

**Fourth: make the adversarial analysis the contribution.** Attackers should know k, the reset rule, the reference window and the per-context thresholds. They should pace below drift, interleave, select contexts, poison baselines and inject prompts. Defenses would include OS-CFAR or trimmed estimators on trusted-generated reference data, randomized k and h, and game-theoretic per-context allocation with CFAR as the baseline to beat.

A credible project has to clear several bars:

- **Budget-matched strong baselines:** max, second-max, three strikes, top-k sums, Wen-style credence, a risk-control-calibrated per-step threshold, and a full-history LLM monitor.
- **A two-sided rule:** single-step trigger OR accumulated trigger, so single-action sensitivity is never sacrificed.
- **Baselines the untrusted model cannot shape:** never per-agent baselines fit on the agent under suspicion.
- **Real LLM-monitor score streams:** not synthetic copulas.
- **Gains that survive monitor-aware, time-aware, selective attackers:** anything less reads as an aggregation ablation.

## Conclusion

The research question has shifted. "Has anyone applied CUSUM/SPRT to AI monitors?" now has the answer yes: in adjacent domains with full rigor, and in AI control on synthetic data. The live question is whether **anything about the control threat model makes these tools work better than the max-plus-quantile baseline once the attacker knows them**. The most likely honest answer splits the idea in two. The CFAR half is valuable mainly as **audit-budget accounting**: honest FPR held constant across contexts and over time, which deployments need regardless of detection power. The sequential half is valuable mainly for **diffuse, cross-instance threats where harm needs many steps to land**, which is the incrimination problem GDM and AISI keep naming without solving. Neither half is a general detection-power win, and pitching either as one invites the strongest objections above.

Timing matters as much as framing. Problem statements from GDM, AISI, ControlConf and LessWrong agendas already exist. Distributed-attack testbeds already exist. The statistical machinery is off the shelf, and at least one preprint has staked the "first formal treatment" claim. The remaining window is plausibly months, not years. The cheapest high-value first move is the heterogeneity and autocorrelation measurement on existing LinuxArena and SHADE-Arena honest runs. It decides whether the CFAR half is worth building, and it produces a reportable result either way.
